Structural Health Monitoring of Highway Bridges for Early Infrastructure Deterioration Detection

How continuous SHM enables early deterioration detection and data-driven maintenance for aging highway bridge infrastructure.

Jul 16, 2026 Case Study
Padmini Vykuntam | Sentra Technologies

Executive Summary

Highway bridges are among the most valuable assets in any transportation network, yet many continue to be managed through periodic inspections that provide only limited visibility into their structural condition. As traffic volumes increase and infrastructure ages, asset owners require continuous insight into how bridges perform under real-world operating conditions.

Structural Health Monitoring (SHM) addresses this challenge by combining intelligent sensors, automated data acquisition, and advanced analytics to monitor structural behaviour in real time. Rather than relying solely on scheduled inspections, SHM enables engineers to detect subtle changes early, support predictive maintenance, and make informed decisions based on actual asset performance.

This case study demonstrates how Sentra implemented a comprehensive Structural Health Monitoring solution on a major highway bridge, enabling continuous bridge monitoring, improving infrastructure asset management, and helping the asset owner transition from reactive maintenance to a data-driven, condition-based maintenance strategy.

Project Background

The bridge in this project is a four-lane reinforced concrete highway bridge that has served as a major transportation corridor for more than three decades. Carrying approximately 55,000 vehicles each day, including a significant volume of heavy freight traffic, the structure experiences continuous dynamic loading far beyond the conditions anticipated during its original design.

Routine bridge inspections consistently classified the bridge as structurally sound, with only minor weathering and localized surface deterioration. However, these inspections represented isolated snapshots of the bridge’s condition and provided little information about how the structure responded to changing traffic patterns, environmental conditions, or material ageing between inspection cycles.

To strengthen its infrastructure asset management strategy, the asset owner sought a continuous Structural Health Monitoring solution capable of delivering real-time engineering intelligence. The objective was to establish a reliable performance baseline, improve long-term bridge monitoring, and support proactive maintenance planning based on measurable structural behaviour.

Challenges of Monitoring Ageing Highway Bridges

Managing ageing bridge infrastructure requires more than periodic visual inspections. While conventional inspections identify visible defects such as cracks, corrosion, or surface deterioration, they cannot continuously monitor structural responses or detect gradual changes that develop over time.

Several factors increased the need for continuous monitoring:

  • Increasing Traffic Loads: Higher freight volumes and heavier axle loads subjected the bridge to millions of additional loading cycles each year, making continuous performance monitoring essential.
  • Environmental Exposure: Seasonal temperature changes, rainfall, humidity, and water ingress contributed to concrete deterioration, reinforcement corrosion, and material fatigue.
  • Material Ageing: After more than thirty years of service, natural ageing mechanisms such as creep, shrinkage, and fatigue could gradually influence structural behaviour.
  • Maintenance Planning: Fixed inspection schedules often resulted in conservative maintenance decisions, unnecessary interventions, or delayed repairs due to limited performance data.

The infrastructure owner required a monitoring solution capable of providing continuous structural intelligence, identifying early signs of deterioration, and supporting predictive maintenance throughout the bridge’s remaining service life.

Objectives of the Structural Monitoring Project

The project aimed to implement a comprehensive Structural Health Monitoring framework that would provide continuous visibility into the bridge’s structural performance. Key objectives included:

  • Establish continuous monitoring of critical structural components
  • Measure structural response under operational traffic loads
  • Capture performance data between routine inspection cycles
  • Detect early indicators of infrastructure deterioration
  • Support condition-based and predictive maintenance
  • Improve engineering decision-making using real-time structural data
  • Extend asset lifespan while maintaining safety and operational reliability

Structural Health Monitoring Solution Overview

Sentra deployed an integrated Structural Health Monitoring solution that combined intelligent field sensors, automated data acquisition, cloud connectivity, and engineering analytics into a unified monitoring platform.

Unlike traditional inspection programs, the system continuously measured the bridge’s response to operational loads and environmental influences. Structural data was automatically collected, processed, and transmitted to a centralized dashboard, allowing engineers to assess asset performance in near real time.

Historical trend analysis enabled engineers to compare current behaviour with baseline performance, helping distinguish normal operational movements from abnormal structural responses. Automated alert thresholds provided early notifications whenever monitored parameters exceeded predefined engineering limits, allowing timely investigation before issues developed into critical failures.

The monitoring platform also strengthened long-term infrastructure asset management by providing objective performance data for maintenance planning, lifecycle forecasting, rehabilitation prioritisation, and future engineering assessments.

Technologies Used

The Structural Health Monitoring system incorporated multiple sensing technologies to capture critical engineering parameters across the bridge.

Sensor Type Function

Strain Sensors

Continuously measured stress variations in primary structural members, enabling engineers to assess load distribution and identify unusual stress concentrations

Tilt Sensors

Monitored minute angular movements in bridge piers and supports, providing early warning of settlement or structural displacement

Displacement Sensors

Measured vertical and horizontal movement under operational loading, allowing long-term tracking of structural behaviour

Environmental Sensors

Recorded temperature, humidity, and other climatic conditions, helping engineers distinguish environmental effects from structural anomalies

All monitoring devices were connected to intelligent data loggers that automatically collected and transmitted information to a secure cloud-based platform. Engineers accessed interactive dashboards displaying live sensor data, historical trends, environmental conditions, and automated alerts, providing a comprehensive view of the bridge’s structural health.

Implementation Phases

The Structural Health Monitoring system was implemented through a structured engineering approach designed to ensure accurate data collection while minimizing disruption to bridge operations.

Phase 1 — Structural Assessment

The project began with a detailed engineering assessment of the bridge. Existing design drawings, historical bridge inspection reports, traffic data, environmental conditions, and maintenance records were reviewed to identify critical structural components requiring continuous monitoring. This assessment established the baseline for sensor placement and system configuration.

Phase 2 — Sensor Deployment

Sensors were strategically installed on primary girders, bridge piers, bearings, expansion joints, and deck sections exposed to the highest operational loads. Each sensor was calibrated and validated to ensure long-term accuracy and reliability under field conditions.

Phase 3 — System Integration

All monitoring devices were connected to a centralized monitoring platform through intelligent data loggers and secure communication networks. Engineering dashboards, automated alerts, and performance thresholds were configured based on design criteria and expected structural behaviour.

Phase 4 — Continuous Monitoring

Following commissioning, the bridge entered continuous operation. Structural responses were monitored 24/7, providing engineers with uninterrupted visibility into bridge performance under varying traffic and environmental conditions. This established a scalable monitoring framework that supports long-term infrastructure asset management and informed maintenance planning.

Results from Continuous Structural Monitoring

Within the first year of operation, the Structural Health Monitoring system generated a comprehensive dataset describing the bridge’s structural behaviour under real-world operating conditions. Unlike periodic inspections, which capture isolated observations, continuous monitoring provided engineers with a complete record of structural performance throughout the day and across changing seasons.

Traffic Load Performance

The collected data confirmed that the bridge responded predictably to normal traffic loads and remained within its design limits. However, continuous monitoring also revealed that certain girders experienced consistently higher strain levels during peak freight movement. Although these values remained within acceptable engineering thresholds, the findings highlighted the long-term influence of increasing commercial traffic and provided valuable input for future maintenance planning.

Environmental Behaviour

Monitoring data showed clear correlations between environmental conditions and structural movement. Temperature fluctuations caused expected thermal expansion and contraction, while prolonged rainfall influenced bearing behaviour and support movement. By analysing structural and environmental data together, engineers were able to distinguish normal seasonal behaviour from potential structural concerns.

Long-Term Performance Trends

Historical trend analysis indicated that the bridge maintained stable structural performance overall. At the same time, gradual variations in strain and displacement were observed in selected structural components. While these changes did not represent an immediate safety risk, they served as early indicators of infrastructure deterioration that warranted continued observation and future maintenance planning.

Engineering Evaluation of Bridge Performance

One of the greatest advantages of continuous Structural Health Monitoring is the ability to replace assumption-based decisions with measurable engineering evidence.

Instead of relying solely on periodic inspection reports, engineers gained access to continuous performance data that allowed them to:

  • Evaluate actual structural behaviour under operational loading
  • Compare measured performance against original design assumptions
  • Assess the impact of increasing freight traffic on critical structural elements
  • Quantify the effects of temperature and environmental conditions
  • Identify gradual performance changes before visible deterioration occurred

This data-driven approach enabled engineers to prioritize maintenance activities based on actual asset condition rather than fixed inspection schedules. Maintenance resources could be directed toward components showing measurable changes, improving efficiency while reducing unnecessary interventions.

In addition, the historical database created by the monitoring system now serves as a valuable reference for future inspections, rehabilitation projects, lifecycle assessments, and long-term infrastructure planning.

Impact of Structural Health Monitoring Solutions

The implementation of Sentra’s Structural Health Monitoring solution delivered significant operational and engineering benefits for the asset owner.

  • Early Detection of Deterioration: Continuous monitoring enabled engineers to identify subtle structural changes before they developed into visible defects or costly repair requirements.
  • Improved Asset Management: Real-time structural data provided objective information for maintenance planning, rehabilitation prioritisation, and lifecycle management, supporting more confident engineering decisions.   • Predictive Maintenance: By continuously monitoring structural performance, maintenance activities could be scheduled according to actual asset condition rather than predetermined inspection intervals, reducing unnecessary maintenance while improving reliability.
  • Condition-Based Maintenance: Continuous monitoring supported the adoption of condition-based maintenance, allowing maintenance budgets and engineering resources to be focused where they delivered the greatest value.
  • Enhanced Operational Safety: Automated alerts enabled rapid investigation whenever monitored parameters exceeded predefined thresholds, strengthening risk management while maintaining uninterrupted bridge operations.
  • Extended Asset Lifespan: Early intervention and informed maintenance decisions helped reduce long-term deterioration risks, contributing to a longer operational life and improved return on infrastructure investment.

Conclusion

Modern infrastructure cannot be managed effectively using periodic inspections alone. Bridges are continuously exposed to changing traffic patterns, environmental conditions, and material ageing, all of which influence structural performance between inspection cycles.

Structural Health Monitoring closes this information gap by delivering continuous, real-time insights into asset behaviour. By combining intelligent sensing technologies with advanced analytics, infrastructure owners gain the information needed to detect deterioration earlier, improve maintenance planning, optimize lifecycle costs, and make more informed engineering decisions.
